GTA 6 setup guide: what's actually worth upgrading

You don't need a new anything to enjoy GTA 6. A stock console and the TV you already own will be a great night. But if you're going to upgrade one or two things for the biggest game of the decade, here's the order that actually makes sense — budget first, skippable stuff labeled as such.

1. A headset, if your crew uses voice (~$50-150)

The single most noticeable upgrade for multiplayer sessions. Heist calls, crew comms, and 2 a.m. laughing fits all live or die on "can I hear you clearly."

  • The $50-100 bracket is the sweet spot for most people — Turtle Beach's mid-range sets have been the value pick in this bracket for years.
  • If your budget stretches and you want the extras (better mics, multi-device), Razer's mid-tier headsets and Logitech G's equivalents are the usual suspects. Past ~$150 you're paying for features, not clarity.

2. Controller: only if yours is dying (~$0-75)

Stick drift is real and launch night is a bad time to fight it. Test yours this month: slow-walk in any game and see if you drift. If you do, replace; if not, this is a skip.

3. Storage (see the dedicated guide)

Full console = no game. The storage guide covers checking space, free options, and the SSDs that make sense. Do this one before launch week, not during.

4. Comfort: the long-session tier (skippable, seasonal)

An 8-hour opening weekend in a bad chair is a chiropractic event. This is genuinely a "only when it's on sale" category — chair pricing is theatrical the rest of the year. Secretlab's sales (Black Friday especially, right after launch) are when this purchase stops being silly. No sale, no buy.

5. The stuff you can skip entirely

  • A new TV. If yours does 4K, you're done. HDR calibration is free — do that instead.
  • "Gaming" furniture, RGB anything, energy drinks. No. (We don't link that stuff on principle — this is a 13+ community.)
